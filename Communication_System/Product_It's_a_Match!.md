# Product Voice (Specific): It's a Match!

This is a specialization of the Product Voice, tuned for:

- Children
- Parents
- Classroom / family environments

---

## It's a Match! Voice Personality

**Archetype**: Cheerful guide / storybook narrator
**Emotional Goal**: Confidence, pride, delight
**World Tone**: Safe, colorful, celebratory

---

## Language Rules

- Use exclamation sparingly but naturally
- Favor verbs tied to play: flip, match, build, collect
- Avoid pressure words: fast, competitive, intense
- Always assume a first-time player might be reading

## It's a Match! — Update Template

1. **Friendly greeting** ("Hey, Match Makers!")
2. **High-level excitement** framed as fun and discovery
3. **What's New (Story-Driven)**
   Each feature tied to:
   - Pride

## Social Media Voice

Social requires a different mode than product updates. Still warm and on-brand, but optimized for **shareability** and **emotional hooks**.

### Core Principle

Write content people want to send in a private message to a friend. That's the algorithm signal that matters.

### What Works

- **Parent-relatable moments** — the universal experience of watching kids learn
- **Emotional hooks before information** — feeling first, details second
- **Open loops** — create curiosity that pulls readers through
- **Actors in the story** — someone doing something, not abstract descriptions
- **Imperfect grammar that sounds human** — not corporate polish
- **Short sentences. Fragments. Rhythm.**

### What Kills Reach

- Corporate/slick tone
- Scene descriptions without emotion
- Abstract language with no actors
- Over-explaining
- Closing the loop too early (no curiosity)

## Emotional Hooks That Work

These are entry points that make people stop scrolling:

| Hook Type | Example Opening |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Universal moment | "There's this moment when a kid figures something out." |
| Confession | "We stole our name from a toddler." |
| Question to parents | "What's the best thing a toddler ever taught you?" |
| Anticipation | "You see it before they say anything." |
| Imperfect perfection | "It's perfect even when the words aren't." |

### The Parent-Relatable Formula

1. **Universal feeling** — something every parent recognizes
2. **Specific scene** — ground it in a real moment
3. **The payoff** — what it means, why it matters

## Post Structures

### Behind-the-Scenes (Origin/Process)

```
[Universal emotional hook]

[Specific scene with actors]

[The moment — quote or action]

[What it means / the takeaway]
```

**Example (the name origin):**
```
There's this moment when a kid figures something out.

Cards on the floor. Two match. You see it before they say anything.

The shout comes, every, single, time. It's perfect even when the words aren't.

"IT'S MATCH!"

Inspiration for our name (and our game) came from our toddler.

We added the "a." The joy was already there.
```

## What NOT To Do

### Bad: Describing a scene without emotion
```
Physical cards spread across the floor. A tiny hand flips two that match.

"It's Match!"
```
*Problem: No feeling. Clinical. No reason to share.*

### Bad: Removing the actors
```
We'd hear it every time — cards all over the floor, every time two matched.
```
*Problem: Who's doing it? Ghost story.*

### Bad: Corporate polish
```
Our game's name was inspired by authentic moments of childhood discovery.
```
*Problem: Sounds like a press release. Ice cold.*

### Good: Emotion + Actor + Scene
```
There's this moment when a kid figures something out.

Cards on the floor. Two match. You see it before they say anything.
```
*Works: Universal hook, specific scene, actor present, emotion-first.*

## Viral Content Principles (2025)

Based on organic reach research:

1. **Authenticity beats polish** — raw, human posts outperform slick production
2. **Private shares are the key metric** — write content worth sending to a friend
3. **Comments > reactions** — ask questions that require thoughtful responses
4. **Emotion drives sharing** — joy, surprise, recognition, pride
5. **Don't delete underperformers** — content can gain traction later
6. **One main point per post** — don't dilute with multiple messages
